How it works

Every session is a practice plan.

Athletes already know how to get better: show up, run the drill, watch the film. The Assist uses the same routine for school.

01 2–5 min

Warm-up

A few fast questions find where you are today. The tutor adjusts the rest of the session from your answers.

02 10–15 min

Main set

One skill, worked step by step with a tutor that explains things in your language. Sports examples, not textbook filler.

03 5 min

Film review

Past mistakes come back until you get them right. Coaches and parents get a weekly summary, not a wall of data.

Eligibility tracker

16 core courses. No surprises senior year.

NCAA Division I eligibility runs on 16 core courses and a minimum core GPA, with 10 of them locked in before your seventh semester. The Assist tracks every one, so a missed class never costs you an offer.

What makes it different

Tutors who look like you. Lessons with rhythm.

First release

Animated tutors with a real cast

Kids work harder for mentors they recognize. The Assist's tutors are consistent animated characters, designed with Black and diaspora students at the center, built on the same character pipeline I use in professional animation.

Every answer is checked by a second AI before a student sees it. A tutor that teaches the wrong thing is worse than no tutor, so accuracy checks ship on day one.

The tutoring gap

Some families buy a tutor. Most can't.

Families with money have always bought their kids an edge: a private tutor two nights a week, a full test-prep package, someone checking every grade. The students I tutored in Mason never had that. Neither do most athletes who spend their evenings at practice.

The Assist gives every student coached, one-on-one sessions for a small fraction of the cost. A great human tutor is still worth it if you can afford one. The Assist is for everyone who can't.
